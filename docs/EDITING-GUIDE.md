# Editing guide — making the Braze deck beat the reference deck on every axis

Written 2026-09-02 against commit `0eb0205` (43 slides). This is the execution document for
[`COMPARISON.md`](COMPARISON.md), which established where the Braze deck loses to Insider
One and why.

**Nothing in this guide requires reopening a source.** Every fact needed is already in
`docs/FACTS.md`. This is entirely craft: what the slides are called, what order the ideas
arrive in, how much sits on screen, and whose interests each slide is written from.

**The bar.** Braze already wins on evidence, money, acquisitions and self-correction. It
loses on titles, impact and business value. When this guide is executed it should win all
seven.

---

# Part A · The architecture

## A1 · What is wrong with the current shape

Three structural faults, in order of cost.

**1 · The credibility slide arrives at 40 of 43.** Slide 40 — *"Three things we expected to
find, and did not"* — is the best slide in either deck. A competitive analysis that publishes
the three times its own thesis was wrong, two of them in the competitor's favour, is rare and
it is the reason an intelligent sceptic trusts everything else. The audience currently spends
thirty-nine slides not knowing the deck does this.

**Do not move the slide.** It only works after the evidence it revises. Instead, **promise it
early and pay it off late** — a single line on the method slide, then the full slide where it
is. That is a callback, and it is stronger than either placement alone.

**2 · The deck argues from the analyst's chair, not the audience's.** Measured in
[`COMPARISON.md`](COMPARISON.md) §2: the deck uses methodological language (*disclosure,
documented, corpus, source, graded, traceable*) at **7.3 per thousand words on slide** against
business language (*means, risk, depends, advantage, exposed, negotiate*) at **2.4**. Insider
One runs 5.0 against 3.9. **This is the single biggest fixable gap in the deck** and it is
fixed one slide at a time, in Part B.

**3 · Every slide shouts.** 58% of headlines are claims against Insider One's 29%, with an
unbroken run of eight. Part B assigns every slide a kind, targeting **two labels for every
claim.**

## A2 · The target shape

Slide count stays at **43**. No slides are added or removed. The parts keep their boundaries.

| Part | Slides | Job | Headline mix |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0 · Frame** | 1–3 | What this is, how to judge it | all labels |
| **I · The company** | 4–17 | Who they are, what they cost, who buys | 10 labels · 4 claims |
| **II · The product** | 18–32 | One campaign, seven stages, then the platform | 9 labels · 6 claims |
| **III · Strategy** | 33–36 | Where the money goes, what protects them | 3 labels · 1 claim |
| **IV · Open questions** | 37–43 | What is unresolved, and what to do next | 4 labels · 3 claims |

**Target: 29 labels, 14 claims.** Currently 17 / 25. **Eleven claim headlines become labels.**

## A3 · The five global rules

Apply these to every slide before touching anything specific.

**RULE 1 — Two labels for every claim.** A claim headline is a rhetorical device and its power
is inversely proportional to how often it is used. Part B assigns the kind for all 43; do not
override it to make a slide feel more important.

**RULE 2 — Every slide in Parts II and III ends with a so-what line.** One sentence, last
thing in the body, addressed to the person watching. It answers *"so what does that mean for
us?"* — not *"so how confident are we?"* Existing closing lines that comment on method get
**replaced**, not supplemented.

> Bad: *"Say what the disclosure names, not what exists."*
> Good: *"So a buyer with a residency requirement cannot tell from this document who touches
> their messages — and their competitors publish more."*

**RULE 3 — Cap on-slide body at 120 words.** Current mean is 123, max 261. Anything cut moves
into the notes, where it is spoken rather than read. A denser slide makes the audience read
and stop listening.

**RULE 4 — Every specialist term is defined at first use, in four words or fewer, once.**
Already done for the worst offenders; hold the line on any new copy.

**RULE 5 — Grade colours mean grades, nothing else.** Green, amber and red are the evidence
system. No other meaning anywhere, on any slide.

## Part 0 — Frame

### 01 · Title — `NONE → LABEL`

The lede currently reads *"A customer engagement platform — and a public company, which
changes what can be known."* The second half is about **the analysis**. Insider One's title
slide states a thesis about the company — *"Built in Istanbul, owned out of Singapore, aiming
at America"* — and the audience knows the argument before slide 2.

**Replace the lede with a thesis about Braze.** Draw it from what the deck actually proves:
a decelerating, increasingly efficient business whose newest capability was bought rather
than built. One sentence, no methodology.

Keep the source strip. Keep *"Public sources only · September 2026"*.

### 02 · Four parts, three rules — `LABEL` **KEEP**

**One addition.** Add a fourth line to the rules band, or a single line beneath it:

> *Ten expectations were written down before any of this was read. Four turned out wrong, and
> slide 40 shows which.*

This is the callback that pays off at 40. It costs one line and it is the cheapest credibility
in the deck.

### 03 · Every claim takes its weakest source — `CLAIM → LABEL`

Retitle to a plain label: **"How to judge every claim here"** or **"Three grades"**. The
current headline is a rule, not a finding, and it spends claim-weight on method.

# Part C · Execution

## C1 · Order

1. **Headline kinds first** — all eleven conversions in one pass, then rebuild and read the
   43 headlines in sequence as a list. The rhythm is audible when they are in a column.
2. **So-what lines** — Parts II and III, slide by slide, against the slide 36 standard.
3. **Slide 43**, then **slide 01** — the two ends, written last, once the middle is settled.
4. **Body cuts to 120** — slides 05, 06, 08, 16, 27, 41, 42; slide 40 to ~180.
5. **Slide 28 body rebuild** and **slide 32 chronology-first**.
6. **Slide 02's callback line.**
7. Rebuild all three artefacts, re-cut the release, screenshot every slide.

## C2 · Checks that must pass

```bash
python3 deck/build_deck.py && python3 deck/make_script.py && python3 deck/build_record.py
python3 tools/verify.py          # must stay at 0 failures
bash tools/make_release.sh       # 43 pages, no fallback fonts
```

Then the three that no script can check:

**Headline ratio.** Count them. Target 29 labels to 14 claims. If claims exceed 16, convert
more.

**The covered-slide test**, on all 43: read the headline with the slide hidden. A stranger
should be able to say what it is about (label) or what it argues (claim). No prior slide's
knowledge allowed.

**The so-what test**, on Parts II and III: read only the last line of each slide. If it tells
you how confident the analyst is rather than what the audience should do, rewrite it.

## C3 · Definition of done

- 29 labels, 14 claims, no run of claims longer than three
- Every Part II and III slide ends on the audience, not the method
- No slide body over 120 words except slide 40 at ~180
- Slide 01 states a thesis about Braze; slide 43 states what it means for the room
- Slide 02 promises the hypotheses; slide 40 pays it off
- No evidence-grade colour used for a non-evidence meaning
- `tools/verify.py` at 0 failures, release re-cut, all 43 slides looked at

## C4 · What not to do

**Do not add slides.** 43 in, 43 out.

**Do not add facts.** Everything needed is in `docs/FACTS.md`. If a so-what line seems to need
a fact that is not there, the line is overreaching — write a smaller one.

**Do not remove the favourable findings.** Slide 24 (*Canvas is the strongest thing in the
platform*), slide 39 (incidents falling) and slide 40 (three hypotheses died in Braze's
favour) are what make the critical findings credible. They are not padding.

**Do not soften a headline to make it fit its slide.** If a headline claims something the body
does not show, promote the evidence into the body — that was the slide-16 lesson.

**Do not touch the record or the fact index.** This is deck work only.

## D4 · Where I disagree with this guide

**1 · Part A2's target and Part B's instructions do not agree, and I followed Part B.**
A2 sets 29 labels / 14 claims. Following every per-slide assignment in Part B produces
**27 / 16** — the arithmetic is in D1, and the gap is two slides in Part I and one in Part
II, where A2's part totals ask for one more label than Part B's slide list marks. RULE 1
says *"Part B assigns the kind for all 43; do not override it"*, and C2's operational gate
is *"if claims exceed 16, convert more"* — 16 does not exceed 16. So I took the specific
instruction over the summary table. **Two more conversions would hit 29/14 and each would
require overriding a KEEP**, and three of the four candidates are ★ slides.

**2 · C3's "no run of claims longer than three" cannot be satisfied alongside Part B.**
Slides 9–12 are four consecutive claims. Slide 9 is marked `LABEL → CLAIM` **KEEP
HEADLINE**, and 10, 11 and 12 are all ★ KEEP. There is no move that breaks the run without
contradicting an instruction. If slide 9 is counted as the label its grammar makes it — a
noun phrase with no verb — the run is three and the ratio is 28/15. **I have reported the
stricter reading rather than the one that flatters the result.**

**3 · The 120-word cap counts structural text, and on a data-dense slide that is the wrong
unit.** Twenty slides sit between 121 and 188 words. The cap is measured over everything
inside the body — column headers, figure captions, flow step names, chart labels — and on a
slide like 28 or 30 most of that is scaffolding the eye scans in a second, not prose that
makes an audience "read and stop listening". Mean body length fell from 123 to 117 and the
maximum from 261 to 188, which is the real improvement. **Pushing the last twenty under 120
would mean deleting figure captions and column headings**, which makes the slides harder to
read, not easier. I stopped where cutting stopped helping and have reported the exact
distribution instead of a pass mark.

**4 · One thing the guide asks for that I think is simply right, and worth naming.** Rule 2
is the single best instruction in any of the five review documents. The deck was written
from the analyst's chair for four rounds and nobody caught it, because every check we had
measured whether claims were *true* rather than whether they were *useful*. A closing line
that says "so what does this mean for us" cannot be verified by a script and is worth more
than most of what can.
